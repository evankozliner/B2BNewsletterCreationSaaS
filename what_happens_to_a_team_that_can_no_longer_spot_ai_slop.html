An SOP that never says when to clean

Each issue, we create a sample of regulated-industry writing and show how a few changes make it clearer.

 

Original

Investigation in all the aforementioned missed cleaning entries found that the language used in SOP-57 to outline routine cleaning is poorly worded. The SOP states that cleaning is "recommended every 6 months at a minimum." This is deemed to be not sufficiently specific. Furthermore, there is a direction for the analyst to coordinate with IT to disable alarms during cleaning. This direction has proved to be a hindrance to initiating cleaning efforts. Recent communications with the administrator revealed that this is not the current practice. It is apparent that a revision of SOP-57 is necessary to align with current practices at our company.

 

Rewrite

SOP-57 is unclear and incorrect, based on our investigation for the following reasons:

  • How often cleaning is to be performed should be more specific; for instance, cleaning is to be performed every January and June.
  • The SOP states that IT must be contacted to disable alarms; however, the administrator confirms that this is unnecessary.

Recommendations:

1. Rewrite SOP-57 so that it states that cleaning is to be done every January and June.

2. Delete the requirement to coordinate with IT to disable the alarm.

 

Why it works

The original diagnoses the same problem twice. Is the language "poorly worded" or "not sufficiently specific"? Pick one. And "not sufficiently specific" is interpretive anyway: specific enough for whom? "Aforementioned" is legalese. The second half spreads a single point across four clauses, when there is a direction, the direction is a hindrance, the administrator says otherwise, and a revision is apparent all belong in one sentence.

 

Notice the structure, too: problem, then the reason it is a problem, then the recommendation. That order buries the ask and invites wordiness on the way there. The rewrite keeps the same keywords, cuts the redundancy, drops "hindrance" for plain words, and puts the recommendations in a numbered list the reader can act on.
